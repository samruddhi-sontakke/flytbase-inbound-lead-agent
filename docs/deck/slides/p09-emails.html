[6:15 – 7:05] Stage 3 writes the reply sequence, and the key point is that this is an inbound reply, not cold outreach. Rodrigo asked us. So I researched how good teams write and follow up, from Seth Godin's permission marketing, Alex Berman, Laura Lopuch and an open marketing-skills library, and turned it into one playbook with a source on every rule. Email 1 goes out the same business hour. It mirrors what he told us: three 24/7 sites, costly and hazardous crews, the Anglo American referral and Q3. It uses one proof point, SQM's own deployment, asks two questions mapped to his MEDDPICC gaps, and offers two call times in Santiago time, with an offer to continue in Spanish. Each follow-up targets a different gap, adds something new and gets shorter, and the last one closes the loop politely. Python checks the measurable rules: length, question count, time slots, banned phrases and signature. The first draft of email 1 was 212 words, so it was rejected and the agent cut it to 192. These are drafts: nothing is sent automatically.
